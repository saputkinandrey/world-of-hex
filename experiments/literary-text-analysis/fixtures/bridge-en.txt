Mara reached the stone bridge and placed a lantern on the wall.

Rowan watched her from the gate. He kept his sword in its sheath.

"Will you open the gate?" Mara asked Rowan.

"No," Rowan answered. "I will not let the soldiers through."

Mara planned to return before sunrise, but she had not yet left the bridge.

The lantern glass was green. Mist lay over the river.

Rowan checked the lock every evening before he went home.

"Last winter I hid three children in the mill," Mara said.

"I heard that Rowan sold the map," said Iven. "I did not see it happen."

"I never betrayed my friends," Rowan said. Nobody answered his claim.

Mara stayed by the gate because she wanted the children to reach safety.

When the bell rang, Iven flinched and dropped his cup.

Rowan and Iven entered the yard. He lifted the lantern.

The man who lifted it was Iven; Rowan had been carrying the blankets.

"They call me Ash," Mara told the guard. "That is my old nickname."

"Ash, take the blankets," Rowan said to Mara.

Mara accepted the blankets but refused the silver coin.

"Can we cross the river tonight?" Iven asked.

"Only after the bell," Mara replied. "Wait beside the mill."

The discussion turned to food. Rowan asked whether any bread remained.

Iven gave Rowan half a loaf and kept the rest for the children.

Years earlier, Rowan had chosen to abandon a paid post rather than arrest his sister.

"He always pays his debts," Mara said of Rowan. It was her opinion, not the narrator's guarantee.

Iven considered stealing a horse, then decided against it.

A broken cart stood beside the empty stable. Its wheels were covered in dust.

At dawn Mara entered the mill alone. She found an unsigned letter on the table.

The letter said, "I moved the children before midnight." No name identified its writer.

Mara folded the letter. She did not know whether its claim was true.

Rowan arrived and said, "I meant to warn you, but the gate was locked."

Mara stepped away from him when she heard the news.

"I will search the western road," Iven said. He remained in the doorway.

The rain stopped. The three stood quietly until the bell rang again.
